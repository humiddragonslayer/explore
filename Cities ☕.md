Density is killing your gains.
Seriously. 

Let me tell you about (what used to be) my city. 
Kochi's a nice place. It's quieter, smaller. A nice vibe. But there are annoyances. Like, one of the dudes in my cousin's flat brought his girlfriend over once. And as she stepped out, she was spotted by one aunty. In a matter of minutes, the entire building was talking about it.

And I'm not trying to defend that. But it is an interesting contrast. We're used to cities being so big that no one cares about who's next door. And that's good. Right?

The thing is, cities are dense. And that can be bad.

---
## Part 1: Empathy is necessary. Also we are bad at it.

Think about the fact that friend groups only exist because of cities. You can't have a curated, good vibes only gang in a town. There simply aren't enough people. You can hang with a gang, but there are going to be people you don't really vibe with. You just put up with it, and sort of agree to disagree. 

Not so in cities. You can find the perfect group of people ([Play like 5 seconds of it, or just watch the whole thing, why not](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s2TyVQGoCYo)) that cater to your every need. And it's very nice. 

But unfortunately, we don't exercise empathy unless we're force.d to. And having the 'right' people always with us, telling us what we want to hear can be dangerous over time.

It can lead to us unable to parse any interaction outside that zone of familiarity. Sound familiar? 
([This one is paywalled, but just look at the headline and trust me.](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2018/12/the-sex-recession/573949/) 
[Look at the subheading for this one.](https://www.economist.com/international/2018/01/10/teenagers-are-better-behaved-and-less-hedonistic-nowadays))

Now, you might be like "But Dan, that's because of the internet, and its takeover by a small group of corporations that are slowly choking the life out of us, one unnecessary subscription at a time." And that's a very good point, and I'm proud of you for articulating that so well.

But despite how powerful and pervasive their efforts might be, it only works because at a fundamental level because that homogeneity and lack of confrontation entices us. Even the closest, tight knit friend group will have disagreements, and drama. So what if you could just interact with people that are even more similar to you? That will get outraged at whatever you get outraged at, and with the same intensity?

They wrote the code, but we ran it on our machines and agreed to every update. The algorithms just curated what we see and hear better than we could, because even cities aren't dense enough to reach that level.

Now, this isn't to say that 'social media bad'. Though if you're talking about the usual culprits, that is [true](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QEJpZjg8GuA) and will unfortunately keep getting truer as time goes on. But there are interesting things being done in that space, the Fediverse being the most complete and adopted that I've [seen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R3ptZ1W-FRA&t=12s).

## Part 2: So What's In It For Me?

"Now I get it, it's healthier to be more offline, but I'm just watching the funny videos that people send me. What do you want me to do?" - Dude who frequently watches movies and does not get them

Let me tell you about Dwarf Fortress. It's an amazing little game that has so much depth and emergent gameplay that an update to track alcohol spilling led to cat extinction. ([If you need the context for that one.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VAhHkJQ3KgY))

The dwarves you control have backstories, interests, needs, and sometimes annoyingly, moods. The way the game works, a dwarf having a meltdown can lead to them going on a rampage, killing other dwarves and triggering meltdowns in the dwarves that encounter the deaths. Many a dwarf fortress has fallen to cascading events like [this](https://www.reddit.com/r/dwarffortress/comments/195r9bh/one_person_threw_a_tantrum_in_my_fort_it_caused/). 

And this illustrates the need for something called redundancy. That's when you have multiple components that perform the same task in a system, arranged so that even if one fails, there are still others that can get the job done.

Which is why having one friend group that basically fulfills all of your needs is dangerous. If one person fails, the entire group can fall or get drawn into it like a domino effect. 

Even on a societal scale, if your circle of interactions is populated by people similar to you, they are likely to have similar beliefs, biases and blindspots, even if there are more of them (thanks to how dense we've gotten). Diversifying really does ground us.

## Part 3: Why'd You Bring Up Kochi?

Kochi's a nice small city. It has nice vibes, you know everyone that's part of the scene, and it's mostly good times. 

But the best way to look at Kochi is in the context of Kerala. And the best way to look at Kerala is in the context of India.

Now, in the context of India, everyone can agree that things are bad, politically speaking. Everyone's angry, some just more violently than others. I'm no one to lecture you about it, I try to avoid thinking about it by going on the internet too.

But Kerala's always been relatively different. Not according to the [people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kerala_Story#Premise_and_factual_accuracy) who are angry that they don't have power or cultural relevance there. But it has. We have our hiccups, but we have rallied together more often than not, despite the fact that there's more people to fight with. ![[Pasted image 20250313010912.png]]

Or is that because of it? The fact that there is a more even distribution means that people are forced to interact, to know people that maybe they wouldn't have put effort into if they could rather not. And to know someone is to understand them.

Which illustrates the true way forward. It isn't to shun cities, but to leverage their variety.([How you might see things in that case.](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/EaL6MPWcm1s))

## Part 4: So density is still the devil by default?

Not quite. I kind of left something out in my romantic depiction of Kochi. It's not a very happening place. 

There's a movie called Watusi Zombie that I probably wouldn't recommend to you if I knew you, but it is interesting, and there are worse ways to spend 70 minutes of your life.

It's about a dude who (I think) used to do comedy, and is trying to get back into it. Part of what makes it interesting is that outside of the dates that are quoted in the movie, it could be set in any point in the last 10 years. Of course, part of that's because it's sort of designed that way. But part of it is because Kochi's been constantly reinventing the wheel when it comes to the local scene.

Every time there's momentum, and a community builds itself up, inevitably a majority of the core members and stakeholders end up moving somewhere else, or get discouraged because there isn't a scene in the first place. Hell, I'm one of them.

Confusingly, while polarization and division thrive on density, so does art. And especially specialisation in art. You can't have a comedy scene without enough people that aren't into the music scene, and you can't have a healthy music scene without enough people that are willing to step out in general. So we have to take what we've got, and make the most of it.

---
## Conclusion

Right now, we're in a crisis of polarization. People are going their entire lives without being challenged about their views, their opinions and even their actions. And that's definitely bad.

At the end of the day, it isn't about making more connections, but about prioritising the quality and variety of those we do make, because those connections will last. With great responsibility in how you interact with people, comes great power to learn and enact change and do better.
